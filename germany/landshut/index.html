Landshut

The Gothic Brick Town Half an Hour from Munich Airport, Outside Every Earthquake Zone and Inside Bavaria's Low Tax

Lower Bavaria · Gothic Ducal Town Beside Munich Airport
The Pitch

A ducal capital of wide Gothic street-markets and gabled houses on the Isar, crowned by a hilltop castle and one of the tallest church towers in the world, about half an hour by road from Munich's long-haul airport

Why It's No. 8

The airport town: the closest of the eight to a great US gateway, outside all seismic zones on the official DIBt table, with Bavaria's 3.5 percent purchase tax and a real working town of about 72,000

The Honest Cost

Less known and less of a showpiece than the towns above it, with more commuter town than tourist town about it; Munich-driven prices; and an Isar flood map that must be checked at the address

Getting There

Munich airport about 30 minutes by road and 36 minutes by direct train; Munich central station 46 minutes; Regensburg 38 minutes

THE SCORECARD

Why Landshut Closes the List

Landshut closes the list not because it is weak, but because it is specialised. Its great strength is one that matters enormously to an American owner: it is the closest town on the shortlist to a long-haul hub, about 40 kilometres by road, half an hour's drive or 36 minutes by direct train, from Munich airport, one of Germany's two great US gateways. Around that fact it offers a great deal: ground outside every earthquake zone on the official DIBt table, Bavaria's low 3.5 percent purchase tax, a working town of about 72,000 with its own hospitals and a university of applied sciences, and an old town of wide Gothic street-markets under a castle on a hill. What places it eighth is that it lacks the international fame, the headline landscape and the tourist pull of the towns above it; it is a handsome regional capital that many Germans know better as a commuter town for Munich than as a destination. For a buyer who flies often and wants a beautiful, practical, low-hazard base, that may be exactly right.

A detail worth knowing

The tower of St Martin's in Landshut, about 130 metres high, is widely cited as the tallest brick church tower in the world. It was raised in the fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries, when Landshut was the capital of the rich duchy of Bavaria-Landshut, and it still dominates the town, visible for kilometres across the flat Isar valley. Building it took generations: the church was begun in the late fourteenth century, and the tower was finished only after its first master builder, Hans von Burghausen, had long since died. His memorial is still at the church, and his tower still sets the skyline of the town.

The Five Filters

Landshut, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

HIGH. Outside all seismic zones: every municipality and parcel district of Landshut carries code 99, "outside", on the DIBt table. The Isar is the one thing to check. The Flutmulde, a relief channel built between 1948 and 1955, is what the state water office calls Landshut's most important flood defence: it carries part of an Isar flood around the town centre so that the old town stays dry. Outside its protection, check the address on the state flood map.

Access

HIGH. The best on the list for the US: Munich airport about 40 km by road, half an hour's drive. Direct trains reach the airport in 36 minutes, Munich central station in 46 and Regensburg in 38.

Scenery and Culture

MEDIUM. One of the finest Gothic brick townscapes in southern Germany, a castle, a Renaissance palace and a spectacular historical festival. But the surrounding countryside is gentle farmland, and the town has less international pull than the others.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM. Strong long-term demand from Munich and airport commuters and local employers; more modest holiday demand, with big peaks in festival years.

Practical Living

HIGH. A regional capital of about 72,000 with two hospitals, schools, a university of applied sciences, major employers and every service; Munich within the hour.
