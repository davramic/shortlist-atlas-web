Saint Andrews

A Loyalist Grid on Passamaquoddy Bay, Looking Back at Maine Across the Tide

New Brunswick · Loyalist Resort Town on Passamaquoddy Bay
The Pitch

A 1783 Loyalist town at the mouth of the St. Croix River: a historic district of clapboard houses, a grand railway-era resort, a great garden, whales in the bay, and Maine a short drive away

Why It's No. 7

Outside any CMA or CA, so the federal ban does not apply, in a province with no foreign-buyer surtax: with Knowlton, the cleanest purchase on the list, and the Maritime town closest to the US

The Honest Cost

A small town, 2,048 people in 2021, far below the Atlas's 5,000 floor; a summer economy; the highest building-code earthquake value on this list; a provincial tax on second homes

Getting There

Saint John airport about 130 km, 1 h 30 to 1 h 45, connecting via Canadian hubs; Bangor, Maine about 200 km, some 3 h via the Calais crossing

THE SCORECARD

Why Saint Andrews Ranks Seventh

Saint Andrews ranks seventh on the list and near the top on paper. It lies outside every Census Metropolitan Area and Census Agglomeration, so the federal ban on non-Canadian buyers does not reach it, and New Brunswick levies no foreign-buyer surtax; an American pays the same 1 percent transfer tax as anyone else. It is a beautiful, historic, genuinely distinctive place, a Loyalist town on the Bay of Fundy with a resort-era grandeur, and it is closer to the United States than any other Maritime town in this dossier. It ranks seventh because it is small, well below the Atlas's usual 5,000 floor at about 2,000 people on the 2021 count, and because it is a summer town: much of its economy runs from June to October, and the nearest airport with any real network is more than an hour away. We say that plainly. A buyer who loves it for what it is will not mind.

A detail worth knowing

Some of Saint Andrews' oldest houses arrived by water. When the Loyalists who had settled at Castine, on Penobscot Bay in Maine, learned after the Revolution that the border would leave them on the American side, a number of them dismantled their houses, loaded the timbers onto vessels, and carried them up the coast to rebuild in the new British town at Saint Andrews in 1783. Local tradition holds that several of those houses still stand on the grid, and the town's historical society can point them out on a walk.

The Five Filters

Saint Andrews, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

MEDIUM. The most important hazard fact in this volume: the National Building Code's 2020 seismic value for Saint Andrews, Sa(0.2) of 1.13 g on firm ground, is the highest on this list, about five times Lunenburg's and on a par with Vancouver's. It reflects the Passamaquoddy Bay earthquake of March 1904 and the code's view that the bay is a source zone. Large earthquakes here are rare, which is why the grade is not lower, but an old wooden house with a brick chimney needs a structural eye. Storm surge affects Water Street, and Fundy tides have a large range. Most of the grid rises gently above the shore.

Access

LOW. Saint John airport is about 1 h 30 to 1 h 45 away and Fredericton about 2 h; Fredericton's schedule lists only Toronto, Ottawa, Montréal and Calgary, so Americans connect through Canadian hubs. Bangor, Maine is about three hours away via the border. Driving from New England is the most practical route.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. A National Historic District, a grand resort hotel, a celebrated garden, a tidal island estate you drive to on the seabed, whale-watching, and the Fundy Isles.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM. A strong summer, from the resort, the garden and the whales, and a good early autumn; the town is quiet from November to April.

Practical Living

LOW. Small-town services in town; the hospital and larger shopping are in St. Stephen, about thirty minutes away, and tertiary care in Saint John. Many businesses are seasonal.
