Town Overview

Life in Lyons-la-Forêt

Half-timbered houses gather around the old market hall, with beech forest beyond the village. Lyons-la-Forêt is an intimate choice for quiet weekends and a Normandy second home.

The Appeal

Lyons-la-Forêt is officially one of the most beautiful villages in France, with pink-brick and half-timbered houses around a 17th-century covered market. A royal forest rings the village, and Paris is about an hour and a half away.

Why It Made the Shortlist

This small Normandy village sits in seismic zone 1, France’s lowest band, with low clay exposure in the village center. Worth checking: former underground quarries and ground conditions across the wider municipality.

Worth Knowing

It is a village of about 760 residents, with fewer services than a town. It suits a quieter second-home rhythm, and rental demand comes mainly at weekends and holidays.

Getting There
  • Rouen, with direct trains to Paris, is about 40 minutes away.
  • Paris is about 1 hr 30 min by road.
  • The village sits deliberately off the main roads, which is the point.
A Story From the Place

Lyons-la-Forêt has a genuine footnote in English royal history: King Henry I of England, the Conqueror's youngest son, died here in December 1135, reportedly after eating "a surfeit of lampreys" against his physician's advice. The village was then called Saint-Denis-en-Lyons, and it had long been a hunting seat of the Dukes of Normandy in their royal forest.

Population

Setting
On the river Lieure at the center of the Forêt de Lyons, a roughly 11,000-hectare former royal forest
