How We Screen

Five Filters, No House Sold

We take no commission and list no property. That leaves us free to read each town against the things that go wrong, say so plainly, and keep the cautionary towns in the book.

The Five Filters

01

Hazard

What the national registers say about earthquake, flood, wildfire and unstable ground, at the town's scale, and plainly what they cannot tell you about one parcel.

02

Access

The real way in and out: airports with scheduled service, mainline rail, the roads and the last slow mile, each marked for a live recheck.

03

Scenery and Culture

The specific draw that makes ordinary days worth living there. A strength of the place, never a safety claim.

04

Rental Viability

Whether lawful letting is possible at all, and whether demand outlasts the season. No yield is promised.

05

Practical Living

The life a postcard leaves out: emergency care, schools, markets, broadband, and a town that does not close for winter.

The Mexico dossier adapts the set to its ground: security evidence (the dated State Department advisory and official crime data, at their stated scale), hazard evidence, title and lawful use including the federal restricted zone, access and healthcare, and practical living and culture.

The Evidence Standard

Registers first. Seismic bands, flood and wildfire layers and ground-movement maps come from the competent national source. France: Géorisques. Italy: the Department of Civil Protection classification. Greece: the seismic code and official flood maps. Spain: IGN and MITECO. Portugal: IPMA and the national annex. Mexico: CENAPRED, CONAGUA and the national seismological service. The United Kingdom: the Environment Agency and SEPA flood maps, the British Geological Survey and UKHSA radon maps. Canada: Natural Resources Canada's seismic hazard model and provincial flood and wildfire mapping. Germany: the DIBt seismic-zone assignment and each state's flood maps. The Netherlands: KNMI and the national flood-risk maps. Australia: each state's bushfire-prone land and flood overlays. Japan: the J-SHIS national seismic hazard maps, the MLIT hazard map portal and JMA volcano alerts. South Korea: the Korea Meteorological Administration and national disaster maps. Thailand: the Thai Meteorological Department, the disaster-prevention department and the national air-quality network.

At the source's own scale. A municipal seismic band describes a municipality, not your walls. The dossiers never turn a regional map into a verdict on one house, and they never declare a whole town safe.

Dated, and marked when mutable. Prices, timetables, airline routes, tax thresholds, visa rules and programme status change. Each carries its audit date and a note to recheck it with the operator or authority.

Two clean rounds before release. Each edition is rebuilt from source and must pass two consecutive clean quality rounds, facts, structure, typography and a page-by-page visual review, before it replaces the previous one.

What the Order Means

Each dossier puts its towns in an order so they can be compared. The order is editorial. It is not a safety certificate, a prediction of price or income, or advice to buy. A good town can contain a bad property, and a difficult town can contain one that works.
