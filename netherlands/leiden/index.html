Leiden

The University City of Canals, Where the Pilgrims Lived Before America

Zuid-Holland · Pilgrim University City Near Schiphol
The Pitch

The oldest university in the Netherlands, a canal city of Rembrandt's birth and great museums, and the town where the Pilgrim Fathers lived for more than a decade before the Mayflower, with Schiphol a short train ride away

Why It's No. 4

It has the best rail access to Schiphol of the eight and a story American buyers already know, in a lively, beautiful city with an academic hospital on its doorstep

The Honest Cost

It stands on low ground in the Holland polders, where flood depth and wooden-pile foundations are real questions, it is crowded with students, and its buy-to-let protection covers the whole city

Getting There

Intercity trains to Schiphol several times an hour in about a quarter of an hour, with night trains round the clock; Amsterdam, The Hague and Rotterdam close by

THE SCORECARD

Why Leiden Is Fourth

Leiden would be first on access alone. Frequent intercity trains reach Schiphol in roughly a quarter of an hour, The Hague is minutes away, and Amsterdam and Rotterdam are an easy ride. It is also one of the most beautiful cities in the country, with a ring of canals, the oldest university in the Netherlands, a botanical garden where Dutch tulips began, and museums of international standing. And it has a story no other town on this list can tell an American: the Pilgrims who sailed on the Mayflower lived here for more than a decade before they left. The reason it is fourth is the ground. Leiden stands on low land in the heart of the Holland polders, with peat and clay underfoot, which makes flood depth and foundations real questions, and its buy-to-let protection covers the whole city. Go in with those facts, and Leiden offers an exceptional life.

A detail worth knowing

In 1574, Leiden was besieged by Spanish troops for months, and its people were starving when the rebels broke the dikes and flooded the land so that ships could sail over the fields to relieve the city. The relief came on 3 October, and Leiden still celebrates it every year with a festival, and with the traditional meal of herring and white bread, the food the relief ships brought, and hutspot, a stew of carrots, onions and potatoes, said to have been found still cooking in the abandoned Spanish camp. The university was founded the following year.

The Five Filters

Leiden, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

LOW to MEDIUM. Leiden stands on low ground in the heart of Holland, behind defences that have historically carried the country's highest protection standard. That standard makes a breach very unlikely, but if one occurred, the polders could flood deeply, and the national flood map at overstroomik.nl gives the modelled depth for every postcode. Peat and clay soils make subsidence and the condition of wooden-pile foundations real questions. No seismic concern.

Access

HIGH. The best rail access to Schiphol of the eight, with frequent intercity trains in roughly a quarter of an hour, and The Hague, Amsterdam and Rotterdam close by.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. Canals, the oldest university in the country, the Hortus botanicus, great museums, Rembrandt's birthplace, the Pilgrim story, and dunes, beaches and bulb fields within a short ride.

Rental Viability

LOW to MEDIUM. Demand is intense, but buy-to-let protection applies citywide for four years after purchase, with limited exceptions, and the municipality states that tourist letting is not allowed in its temporary-letting exception. Plan to live in the house.

Practical Living

HIGH. An academic hospital, a large international research community, every kind of service, and excellent transport. International schools are nearby in The Hague and Wassenaar.
