Maastricht

The Burgundian City on the Meuse, Where the Netherlands Meets Belgium and Germany

Limburg · Burgundian Hill City on Three Borders
The Pitch

The least Dutch city in the Netherlands: Roman and medieval, built of limestone on a river among hills, with vineyards, carnival, a great art fair and Belgium and Germany on the doorstep, at the second-lowest average valuation of the eight

Why It's No. 5

It offers the most distinctive culture on this list and, with Middelburg, the best value, a hill town standing well above sea level with an academic hospital, but it carries two hazards the other towns do not share

The Honest Cost

The Meuse set a discharge record in July 2021, Limburg is the Netherlands' natural earthquake zone, and the local airport is leisure-only, so a transatlantic trip runs through Brussels, Düsseldorf or Amsterdam

Getting There

Direct intercity to Amsterdam in about 2 h 30; Brussels and Düsseldorf airports each about 1 h 20 by road; Liège about 30 min by train

THE SCORECARD

Why Maastricht Is Fifth

Maastricht is the cultural outlier of this volume. Everything that defines the rest of the Netherlands, flat polders, brick gables, dikes, is absent or different here. It is a city of limestone and hills on the Meuse, with Roman foundations, two great medieval churches, a Burgundian love of food and festivity, and Belgium and Germany within a short drive. It is also, with Middelburg, the best value on this list, with the second-lowest average tax valuation of the eight. It ranks fifth because it carries two hazards the other seven do not: the Meuse, which set a discharge record in July 2021 when Limburg flooded, and the natural seismicity of southern Limburg, the country's main zone of tectonic, rather than gas-induced, earthquakes. Its airport access for Americans is also indirect. For a buyer who wants something different and checks the address carefully, Maastricht offers a life unlike anywhere else in the Netherlands.

A detail worth knowing

On 7 February 1992, the leaders of the twelve member states of the European Community met in the provincial government building on the Meuse in Maastricht and signed the Treaty on European Union, which created the EU as it is known today and set the path to the euro. The city's name has been part of European politics ever since, and the building, the Gouvernement, still stands on its island in the river.

The Five Filters

Maastricht, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

LOW to MEDIUM. The centre stands well above sea level, so sea flooding is not the issue. The Meuse is: it set a discharge record of 3,260 cubic metres per second at Eijsden on 15 July 2021, when towns across Limburg flooded, and the city relies on quays and river works rather than a dike ring. Southern Limburg is also the country's natural earthquake zone, and old marl workings lie under the hills. In 2021 the academic hospital came close to evacuation, and a new mobile flood wall for Heugem, Randwyck and the hospital is being tested in October 2026.

Access

MEDIUM. Excellent for Belgium and Germany, but the local airport handles leisure flights only. The transatlantic trip runs through Brussels or Düsseldorf, each roughly an hour and twenty minutes by road, or Schiphol, roughly two and a half hours by train or more.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. The most distinctive culture in the country: Roman and medieval churches, limestone streets, carnival, the TEFAF art fair, vineyards on the hills, and three countries within half an hour.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM. Strong tourism, events and student demand, but student-rental pressure is high and the municipality guards its housing stock closely.

Practical Living

HIGH. An academic hospital, a large university, a lively centre and lower costs than the west, with cross-border shopping and services in Belgium and Germany.
