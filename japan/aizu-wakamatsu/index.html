Aizu-Wakamatsu

The Samurai City of the Red-Tiled Castle, a Hundred Kilometres From the Coast

Aizu, Fukushima · Samurai City and Red-Tiled Castle at Low Prices
The Pitch

A basin city with the deepest samurai history in northern Japan, a castle roofed in red tiles, sake breweries, lacquer workshops, and some of the lowest prices of any real city on this list

Why It's No. 6

A 3.2% J-SHIS chance of intensity 6-lower shaking in 30 years, the Aizu region's only tertiary critical care centre, and Tokyo in under three hours by rail via Koriyama

The Honest Cost

The Fukushima name, though the city is 98 km inland from the Daiichi site; Mt Bandai, 18 km away at alert level 1 with slightly raised earthquake counts; snowy winters; soft land prices

Getting There

Ban'etsu West Line rapid to Koriyama in about 1 h 5, then the Tohoku Shinkansen, about 1 h 22 to Tokyo

THE SCORECARD

Why Aizu-Wakamatsu Ranks Sixth

Aizu-Wakamatsu is the town on this list that most buyers will skip because of one word in its prefecture's name, and that is exactly why it deserves a careful look. On the ground test it does well: J-SHIS, the national seismic model, gives the castle a 3.2 percent chance of intensity 6-lower or greater shaking in 30 years in its 2024 edition. It sits in the Aizu basin, far inland in the west of Fukushima Prefecture, 98 km in a straight line from the Daiichi nuclear site on the Pacific coast, with mountains between, and its air dose rate reads 0.047 to 0.059 microsieverts an hour on the prefecture's monitors. It has a samurai history as dramatic as any in Japan, a tertiary emergency centre, a university, and prices that are low for a city of 109,000. Its honest costs are a volcano on the horizon, a snowy winter, a market that is slowly sliding, and a stigma that will shape resale and rental whatever the facts. Sixth place reflects all of that.

A detail worth knowing

Tsuruga Castle's keep wears red tiles. The castle was roofed with red-glazed tiles at the end of the Edo period, and when the concrete keep of 1965 was reroofed in 2011, the city went back to red tiles like those of the 1860s. In winter, red roof on white snow, it is one of the most striking castle views in the country.

The Five Filters

Aizu-Wakamatsu, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

for Japan, and the basin is far inland, with no tsunami. Mt Bandai, 18 km north-east, is at JMA alert level 1; earthquake counts under it have run slightly high since November 2022, with a one-day burst on 1 December 2025, and the agency sees no sign of eruption. Its great 1888 collapse went north, away from the city. River flood maps, the Nippashi River lahar zone and snow load are the address checks.

Access

MEDIUM. Tokyo in about 2 h 30 of train time via the Koriyama Shinkansen, which is good. Air is weak: Fukushima Airport, reached through Koriyama, flies only to Osaka Itami, Sapporo and, twice a week, Taipei, so the practical gateway is Tokyo.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. A castle, a samurai story that every Japanese schoolchild knows, a unique spiral pagoda, sake and lacquer, and a region of lakes, thatched post towns and mountain railways.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM. Steady domestic history tourism, school trips and Tohoku touring; international demand is held back by the prefecture's name. Winter is quieter.

Practical Living

HIGH. A regional centre with the Aizu region's only tertiary critical care centre, an 837-bed general hospital with a 24-hour emergency room, the University of Aizu, and full shopping and services.
