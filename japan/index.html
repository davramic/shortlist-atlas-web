The Shortlist

Every Town, the Same Five Questions

1
Tsugaru, Aomori · Original castle keep on some of Japan's quietest ground

Hirosaki

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A real castle town of about 156,000 with one of Japan's twelve surviving original keeps, a samurai quarter, a university and some 2,600 cherry trees round the moats, in the orchards below Mt Iwaki

Read →
2
San'in, Shimane · National Treasure castle city with a reactor nearby

Matsue

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A prefectural capital of canals and moats, with one of five castle keeps in Japan designated a National Treasure, a sunset lake, the house of Lafcadio Hearn, and a tea culture three centuries deep

Read →
3
Hokuriku, Ishikawa · Maeda culture and Shinkansen, over a named fault

Kanazawa

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

Edo castle-town culture few cities outside Kyoto can match: Kenrokuen, four protected historic districts, the Omicho market, living crafts from gold leaf to Kutani, and Tokyo 2 h 30 away

Read →
4
Kyushu, Saga · Castle, bay and tea bowls near Fukuoka Airport

Karatsu

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A castle on a headland above a pine-fringed bay, one of Japan's great tea-bowl traditions, a UNESCO-listed November festival, live squid at Yobuko, and a through train to Fukuoka's airport

Read →
5
Tamba, Hyogo · Quiet castle town seventy minutes from Osaka

Tamba-Sasayama

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A small castle town in a basin ringed by mountains, with preserved merchant and samurai streets, the black soybeans and boar hotpot of Tamba, and one of Japan's Six Ancient Kilns 20 km away

Read →
6
Aizu, Fukushima · Samurai city and red-tiled castle at low prices

Aizu-Wakamatsu

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A basin city with the deepest samurai history in northern Japan, a castle roofed in red tiles, sake breweries, lacquer workshops, and some of the lowest prices of any real city on this list

Read →
7
Shiribeshi, Hokkaido · Canal port on the lowest seismic ground found

Otaru

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

A Meiji and Taisho port of stone warehouses, a gas-lit canal, old bank buildings and glass workshops, climbing steep hills above Ishikari Bay, with sushi, ski slopes and Sapporo all close

Read →
8
Hida, Gifu · Old merchant town below the Northern Alps

Takayama

Natural hazardAccessScenery and cultureRental viabilityPractical living

The archetype of old Japan: timber merchant streets and sake breweries, the only surviving Edo-period district magistrate's office building, a UNESCO-listed float festival, and the Northern Alps

Read →
