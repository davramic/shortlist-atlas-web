Chester

The Walled Roman City on the Dee, Where the Shops Stand on Galleries

Cheshire · Walled Roman City with Medieval Galleried Rows
The Pitch

The most complete circuit of Roman and medieval walls in Britain around a city of galleried medieval streets, on a sandstone ridge above the Dee, two miles from Wales

Why It's No. 3

A real city of about 93,000 at northern English prices, with a hospital, a university and a strong visitor trade, and Manchester Airport about 45 minutes away

The Honest Cost

Larger and busier than the smaller towns on the list, with a retail-heavy centre, and river flooding along the low Dee banks to screen out; English purchase tax at its full weight

Getting There

Manchester Airport about 45 min, with Virgin Atlantic nonstops to New York, Atlanta and Orlando; London Euston from 2 h 03 by direct train

THE SCORECARD

Why Chester Earns Third Place

Chester offers the most history per pound on this list. It is a genuine Roman city, founded as the fortress of Deva, still wrapped in about three kilometres of walls you can walk all the way round, and at its heart are the Rows, two-level medieval shopping galleries found nowhere else in the world. Yet it is priced like the north-west of England, not the south, and it is a working city of about 93,000 with a hospital, a university and a major theatre. The terms: it is bigger and busier than a market town, the centre leans heavily on shopping and visitors, and the river Dee brings a flood risk to its low banks that you must check by address. The access is its hidden strength. Manchester Airport, about 45 minutes away, has Virgin Atlantic nonstops to New York, Atlanta and Orlando, which puts Chester closer to a transatlantic flight than any other northern town in this volume.

A detail worth knowing

Look down from the city walls on the south-west side and you see a racecourse laid out on the river meadow below. That is the Roodee, where racing has been held since 1539, and it is often described as the oldest racecourse still in use in England. It is also one of the smallest, a tight oval of little more than a mile, and on race days the walls themselves become a free grandstand. The land it sits on was once the harbour of the Roman port, silted up long ago as the Dee changed its course.

The Five Filters

Chester, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

MEDIUM to HIGH. Low seismic hazard, marginally higher toward North Wales but still low. The walled centre stands on its sandstone ridge; the risk is the Dee, which floods its low banks in places such as the Groves, which the Environment Agency maps in Flood Zone 3, the Boughton and Handbridge riverside and Saltney. The Dee is tidal up to the weir. Radon is low. Check each address.

Access

MEDIUM to HIGH. Manchester Airport about 45 minutes, with Virgin Atlantic's nonstops to New York, Atlanta and Orlando; London from 2 h 03 by direct train; Manchester and Liverpool by rail. The best US access of the northern towns.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. The full walls, the Rows, the amphitheatre, the cathedral, the river and the racecourse, with North Wales and its castles within the hour. Heritage on a city scale.

Rental Viability

HIGH. A big visitor trade, a racing season, a university, a zoo of national standing and a strong long-let market. Year-round demand, with competition to match.

Practical Living

HIGH. A full city: hospital, university, theatre and cinema, big shops, rail links and motorways. The centre can feel geared to shoppers and day-trippers rather than residents.
