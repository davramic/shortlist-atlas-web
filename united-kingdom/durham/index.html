Durham

The Cathedral and Castle on a Wooded River Peninsula, a World Heritage City at Northern Prices

County Durham · UNESCO Cathedral and Castle on the Wear
The Pitch

A Norman cathedral and castle on a rock in a loop of the Wear, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, in a compact university city on the East Coast Main Line, at some of the lowest prices on the list

Why It's No. 7

One of the greatest buildings in Europe at the heart of a small, walkable city with a hospital, fast trains to London and Edinburgh, and value no southern town can offer

The Honest Cost

A student-dominated centre, cooler north-eastern weather, a coal-mining legacy that makes a mining search essential, and no scheduled US flights from nearby Newcastle

Getting There

London King's Cross from 2 h 37 direct; Edinburgh under two hours by train for its US flights; Manchester and London as the other gateways

THE SCORECARD

Why Durham Ranks Seventh

Durham is built around one of the great sights of Europe. On a high, wooded peninsula in a tight loop of the River Wear stand a Norman cathedral and castle, together a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and around them a small, steep, walkable city of about 50,000 people, with a university, a hospital and a station on the East Coast Main Line with direct trains to London and Edinburgh. It offers that at the lowest prices of any town in this volume. The terms are specific. The centre is dominated by students; the weather is that of the north-east, cooler and greyer than the south; the whole county was coal country, so a mining search and a Coal Authority check are essential; and although Newcastle airport is close, it has no US flights, so the gateways home are Edinburgh, Manchester or London. For a buyer who wants a world-class historic setting and real value, and who is willing to go north for it, Durham is a remarkable proposition.

A detail worth knowing

Durham exists because of a coffin. In the ninth century, Viking raids drove the monks of Lindisfarne to flee their island, carrying the body of their saint, Cuthbert, with them. For more than a century the community wandered the north with the coffin, and in 995, so the story goes, they were guided to a high, wooded rock in a loop of the Wear, where they built a church for the saint. That church became the cathedral, and the settlement around it became the city. Cuthbert's shrine is still behind the high altar, and the Venerable Bede, the first historian of England, lies at the other end of the cathedral.

The Five Filters

Durham, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

MEDIUM. Low seismic hazard, and the peninsula stands well above the river, with flooding confined to the low banks such as Framwellgate Waterside and the Sands, which the Environment Agency maps in Flood Zone 3. The specific risk is ground: County Durham was one of the most intensively mined coalfields in England, the Coal Authority places the whole city inside its coalfield, and parts of the centre east of the river fall in its Development High Risk Areas. A Coal Authority mining search is standard and essential.

Access

MEDIUM. Excellent by rail, with direct trains to London and Edinburgh and Newcastle a quarter of an hour away. But Newcastle airport has no US flights, so the gateways home are Edinburgh by train, Manchester by road, or London.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. One of the finest Romanesque buildings in Europe, a castle that is a university college, wooded riverbanks, and the North Pennines, Hadrian's Wall and the Northumberland coast within reach.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM to HIGH. A big student and academic long-let market, graduation and open-day visitors, and heritage tourism, but a shorter tourist season than the southern towns.

Practical Living

HIGH. A compact city with a hospital, shops, a theatre, good rail links and Newcastle's big-city amenities fifteen minutes away.
