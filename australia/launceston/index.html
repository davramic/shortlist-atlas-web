Launceston

The River City at the Head of the Tamar, with a Gorge in Its Backyard

Northern Tasmania · Georgian River City with Gorge and Jet Airport
The Pitch

A Georgian and Victorian river city of about 81,000 where three rivers meet, Cataract Gorge a walk from the centre, the Tamar Valley wine route on the doorstep and a UNESCO gastronomy designation to its name

Why It's No. 1

The best all-round fit on the list: a real jet airport fifteen minutes out, a mild temperate climate, full regional-city services, and active new-housing fringes at Legana, Prospect Vale and Hadspen where a non-resident American can legally buy new

The Honest Cost

Levee-protected flats that the latest climate modelling says will be overtopped later this century, an island that adds one more flight to every trip home, and an established housing stock closed to foreign buyers until at least mid-2029

Getting There

Launceston Airport about 15 to 20 minutes from the centre, with direct flights to Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane; the US connection runs through Melbourne or Sydney

THE SCORECARD

Why Launceston Leads the List

Launceston wins because it is the one town on this shortlist that does nearly everything well at once. It is old enough to have real character, Georgian warehouses and Victorian shopfronts on a river founded in 1806, and big enough, at about 81,000 people, to carry a referral hospital, a university campus and an airport with jets to three mainland capitals. Its climate is mild in a country where heat is the growing danger, and its fringes are growing, which matters more in this volume than in any other: while Australia's ban on foreign buyers of established homes is in force, the new estates of Legana, Prospect Vale and Hadspen are where an American without residency can actually own. The terms are equally clear. Buy on high ground, not on the levee-protected flats, and understand from day one that the Georgian cottage you fall for is off limits for now.

A detail worth knowing

In 1895 Launceston switched on the Duck Reach power station in Cataract Gorge, which made it one of the first cities in the southern hemisphere to light its streets with hydroelectricity. The ruins of the station still stand in the gorge, a pleasant walk upstream from the First Basin, and the Tasmanian habit of turning river water into power that later remade the island's highlands started here.

Launceston General Hospital, northern Tasmania's public referral hospital, with an emergency department · private hospitals in town
The differentiator
UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy (2021) and a jet airport, in a city where you can still walk from the main street into a gorge

The Five Filters

Launceston, Read Five Ways

Natural Hazard

MEDIUM. The river flats carry a real, documented flood exposure behind levees that Tasmania SES says protect the city today but that published climate modelling shows being overtopped by mid-century. The hills are a different story: buy above the flats and away from the bush edge and the hazard picture is ordinary for Australia, with mild heat.

Access

HIGH. A jet airport about 15 to 20 minutes from the centre with QantasLink, Virgin Australia and Jetstar to Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, plus a Canberra service. The only deduction is the island: every US trip needs a connection on the mainland.

Scenery and Culture

HIGH. Cataract Gorge in the city, an intact Georgian and Victorian centre, the Tamar Valley wine route and two World Heritage convict estates within half an hour. Few Australian cities of this size carry so much in so small a radius.

Rental Viability

MEDIUM. Year-round demand from the hospital, the university and the city's working life, with summer tourism on top. New fringe housing lets less well to visitors than a heritage cottage in town, so the realistic let for a foreign owner is a long-term tenancy rather than a holiday home.

Practical Living

HIGH. A full regional city: the public referral hospital, private hospitals, a University of Tasmania campus at Inveresk, supermarkets, a CBD, Metro Tasmania buses and a busy airport. Complex specialist care means Hobart or a flight to Melbourne.
